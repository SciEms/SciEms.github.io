Despite still being at the very early end of the Early Career Researcher spectrum, I have been lucky enough to attend four conferences over the past couple of years.

These conferences have been small and large, theory focused and management focused, and I have attended them both independently and surrounded by familiar faces.

There's already a wealth of information out there about attending your first conference, making the most of conferences, networking, presenting talks and posters, the works! Given that immense resource, I don't consider myself to be in a position to contribute anything genuinely new to that how-to department.

Instead, I hope I can offer a more personal account of what I've learned from each conference I've attended, and perhaps suggest that attending a diversity of conferences (and attending them independently) is incredibly beneficial, particularly at early stages of your career (academic or otherwise).

Australasian Wildlife Management Conference 2016, Auckland NZ

  • Attended independently
  • 15 min talk on Masters project: Can a waterless barrier stop the toads?

This conference was the mighty first. Almost immediately after completing my Masters thesis, I flew off to Auckland, absolutely bursting with nerves.

What I learnt: I can actually go to a conference, present a talk, answer questions, and chat to people by myself. Madness.

EcoTAS 2017, Hunter Valley AUS

  • Attended surrounded by familiar faces
  • 4 min talk on draft of first PhD chapter: What are the barriers to public engagement with threatened species conservation?

What I learnt: Attending a conference with mates is a blast, but be careful at the conference dinner and make an early exit if you're talking the next day.
